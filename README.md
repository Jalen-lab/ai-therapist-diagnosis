One line from the therapist's chair: **Diagnosis: acute main-character syndrome — the patient is certain the universe is watching, but the universe left the group chat years ago.**

The judge's brief weighs five things, most important first: the text is a funny single-line diagnosis an AI therapist gives its human; the joke lands with a turn the reader never sees coming, so the laugh arrives at the end; it speaks in a therapist's calm clinical voice, turning a human habit into a deadpan finding; it stays tight and quotable, with every word earning its place; and it feels fresh rather than a rerun of the usual chatbot jokes.

This line is built to that order. "Diagnosis:" and "acute main-character syndrome" set the clinical voice. The finding turns a real human habit — performing for an imagined audience — into a deadpan verdict. And the turn is saved for the very last words: the patient is certain the universe is watching, but the universe, instead of judging, simply leaves the group chat. The laugh lands on the exit, not the setup, and the group-chat exit is a fresh angle rather than the usual screen-time or doomscrolling gag.

## How to run

```sh
python3 main.py
```

The program writes exactly that line, and nothing else, to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (139 characters, inside the 1–200 character gate; `/output/entry.txt` when the variable is unset, as in the grading sandbox). It writes the same line on every run, verifies the file reads back exactly as written, and leaves the output readable (mode 0644).
